Puerto Rico closed July 2025 with 3,184,835 residents. It is the first time the
island has come in under 3.2 million since the 1980 census.

The part that changes the conversation is where the loss comes from. For years
the answer was the airport. This year, for the third running, it is not: there
were 32,857 deaths against 17,950 births. Almost two deaths for every birth. Net
migration accounted for 2,779 people, a sixth of the decline.

That matters because the two are fixed in different ways. A net outflow responds
to what happens with jobs, housing or the hurricane that year. A population
burying twice as many as it baptises responds to its own age, and that does not
reverse with one good season.

For anyone selling something, the number to watch is not the total but what it
is made of. A market losing 17,686 people a year, and losing them from the top
of the pyramid, does not buy the same thing next year with 0.6% fewer people. It
buys different things.
